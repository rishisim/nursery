**Bodily inputs for language acquisition: literature review and implications for Nursery**

Prepared October 15, 2026. This is a research review and design recommendation, not an implemented protocol or a report of new experiments.

**Main finding**

There is substantial precedent for encoding touch and proprioception as information-bearing representations. The closest useful recipe for Nursery is a modest temporal encoder over contact and joint state, trained with sensory self-supervision and connected to language learning. Existing work supports this direction, but does not establish that it will raise Nursery's picture-only vocabulary asymptote. That requires a separate transfer test and learning curves.

The most consequential changes are: preserve useful sensory content; learn from sequences; distinguish sensory shortcuts from legitimate category information; and separate multimodal competence from improvement after the body is removed. A scalar hand-location gate cannot adequately test these mechanisms.

**Scope and evidence**

This targeted review spans developmental word learning, robotic sensory grounding, temporal body encoders, tactile foundation models, and missing-modality learning. It uses primary papers, proceedings, and author materials, with a recent-work search through October 15, 2026. It is not a systematic review with exhaustive database coverage. Reported findings were checked against available text, not independently reproduced. Architectural suggestions below are proposals inferred from the literature, not published Nursery results.

For the papers most directly informing the design, I inspected methods in the paper or supplement: Zambelli, Lee, Ngiam, Thomason, Vijayaraghavan, HPT, Touch and Go, TVL, Sparsh, Sparsh-X, FactorCL, and Touch Dreaming. For other entries the evidence is explicitly limited to the accessible abstract or project/publisher description. Publication dates come from the paper/proceedings, not search-engine crawl dates.

**What Nursery currently exposes to the learner**

The active code inspected is in `the repository`. Its current body encoder takes four coarse contact scalars, two grip-closure scalars, and six wrist-coordinate scalars. It averages observations over the frame window, encodes the three signal groups, and reduces them to one learned number controlling a spatial prior near the hand. Soft pooling changes the aggregation of visual region scores; it does not give bodily experience a general representational channel.

The renderer saves considerably richer data: per-hand pose, body joints, and packed contact bits on hand vertices. However, these are sampled once per second from chained recorded motion clips. Swapped-object contact is computed from geometric proximity/penetration, not measured force. The saved body files do not supply pressure, friction, temperature, material compliance, or motor commands. These distinctions constrain what a new encoder can learn.

Local evidence: [body encoder](../../nursery/learner/body.py), [training](../../nursery/learner/train.py), [body export and contact computation](../../nursery/simulator/build.py), [one-second sampling](../../nursery/simulator/sessions.py), [existing design and feature-removal rationale](../body_input_v1_v2.md).

**What the evidence changes about our reasoning**

**A. A useful body encoder need not be large.** The strongest close precedent is an MLP plus recurrent memory. Start there rather than assuming all sensory channels need a pretrained multimodal transformer. Use different small projections for contact and proprioception so their effects remain ablatable. Preserve their information through fusion; compressing everything into a single confidence number predetermines the mechanism under test.

**B. Spatial contact and posture belong together.** Contact on a fingertip is difficult to interpret without the finger's configuration. Conversely, a closed hand does not prove object contact. A joint representation can distinguish related configurations using both. Stable anatomical indexing is legitimate: “index fingertip” is a sensor identity, unlike “object category.” A simple anatomically pooled contact map is a reasonable first compromise between one bit per hand and thousands of raw inputs. This is an engineering proposal, not a claim that a particular pooling resolution is optimal.

**C. Do not remove the property we want to learn.** The existing design flags a body signal as problematic when it predicts a held category and progressively coarsens/noises it. Category prediction may arise from real grasp geometry, from repeated source trajectories, or both. Those possibilities need different tests. Good performance on unseen motions and object instances is desirable. Good performance that disappears after splitting replay families indicates a shortcut. Chance-level body-only classification is not a suitable universal acceptance condition.

The boundary is access: legitimate inputs are the agent's local joint state and contact pattern. Privileged simulator information includes object labels, asset IDs, ground-truth meshes as encoder inputs, and scene annotations unavailable to a body. Keep the latter for diagnostic scoring only. A probe may use labels to measure an encoder without making those labels part of representation training.

**D. Time is information, but the stored time scale matters.** Preserve ordering instead of averaging a five-frame window before encoding. At 1 Hz, we can reasonably investigate slow grasp changes, gross arm motion, and contact persistence. We cannot claim to represent impacts, texture vibrations, or fine slip dynamics. An encoder cannot recover frequencies never recorded. Higher-rate body extraction could later be useful without necessarily rerendering every image, but that is additional work to justify from a diagnostic, not a prerequisite to this review.

The session builder chains distinct source clips. A temporal encoder must reset state or mask prediction targets at those boundaries; otherwise it learns artificial transitions. Different sampling rates also require actual time offsets and validity masks rather than assuming adjacent array rows are equally spaced and continuous. Zero contact and absent body data need distinct encodings.

**E. Teach body structure independently of the noisy name pairing.** A simultaneous body–video pair is useful sensory evidence even when the caregiver is discussing another object. Train a body representation with a sensory objective across such episodes, then allow weak language to associate with the combined experience. A loss that asserts every utterance names the touched object would simply reproduce the current alignment assumption with a larger network.

A small masked-contact/posture objective is a defensible starting proposal. Evaluate it against trivial persistence and mean predictors; predicting a nearly constant grip is not evidence of meaningful representation learning. Future-contact prediction is an alternative, but measured displacement is not an independently chosen action, and observation prediction from replay should not be called causal dynamics learning.

**F. Preserve body-specific information before trying to transfer it.** FactorCL's finding is relevant to the objective, not just network size. If the entire body embedding is forced to imitate frozen image features, information without a visual counterpart can be suppressed. Preserve a body pathway that can represent its own content; use a projection for cross-modal alignment if alignment is tested. A large factorization framework is unnecessary for the first controlled experiment.

This also refines my earlier suggestion to immediately distill a combined teacher into vision. First establish that the combined learner actually learned something useful. A teacher with no advantage offers little justification for another objective. A competent teacher can still contain information impossible to infer from a particular test photograph; avoid treating full latent equality as a guaranteed attainable target.

**G. Multimodal success and transfer success are separate outcomes.** Much of the robotics literature retains tactile/proprioceptive information at inference, or retrieves stored sensory observations of the candidate object. That is legitimate, but different from Nursery's primary picture-plus-word test. We need two measured questions: does access to the body improve grounding; and does prior body experience improve recognition when only vision is available? The first can succeed while the second fails.

Missing-modality training and cross-modal prediction have established precedents. They offer possible transfer mechanisms, not a promise. For an identical test image compatible with two physically different objects, a visual-only system cannot know which unseen physical state is present merely because it had tactile training. Training can improve visual feature learning, priors, and sample efficiency; it cannot conjure missing test-time evidence. This is an information-availability argument, not an empirical result from a Nursery run.

**H. “Ceiling” needs an operational definition.** Keep three ideas separate: convergence with more optimization on fixed data, performance as the amount/diversity of experience increases, and a fundamental limit imposed by the test input. A 40-epoch plateau addresses only the first, imperfectly. A benefit at low data is valuable progress even if asymptotes ultimately match. Conversely, fitting an asymptote from a few still-rising points can mislead. Nursery's claim should remain an empirical comparison under a stated training/data budget.

**I. The benchmark constrains where a benefit can appear.** Noun-to-picture recognition is an appropriate continuity benchmark, but it mostly asks about visually recognizable categories. Many tactile studies choose material, compliance, force, weight, or action properties precisely because extra sensors can inform them. We should keep the present noun evaluation rather than replace it to obtain a positive result. If a body representation works but nouns do not improve, a separately reported, physically supported property/action diagnostic can locate the mismatch. Do not add “soft” or “heavy” to the scientific claim unless corresponding physical variation and sensory evidence exist.

**J. Pretraining can silently change the question.** UniTouch and TVL show effective ways of connecting touch to an existing semantic space. Copying their pretrained language/vision teachers would import extensive external knowledge. Nursery already uses frozen visual features, so current conclusions should be conditional on that prior. Hold pretrained components identical across conditions and avoid presenting the experiment as acquisition from infant experience alone. Borrow the mechanisms without silently borrowing additional semantics.

**A minimal next learner, proposed rather than implemented**

The next implementation should answer: can an information-bearing body sequence improve grounded learning beyond a matched visual learner, and does that benefit survive removal of the body at test?

| Component | Smallest useful proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Proprioception | Retain local finger-joint configuration and body-relative wrist/arm state, with valid temporal differences. Fit normalization on training data only. | Preserve shape and movement information while excluding world-position and identity shortcuts. |
| Contact | Contact over stable finger/palm patches, separately for each hand. Keep no-contact observations and a separate missing-input mask. | Preserve where touch occurs on the body without needing optical-tactile sensors. |
| Temporal encoder | Separate small input MLPs followed by one GRU or LSTM; a 128-dimensional latent is an initial engineering choice, not an optimum. Reset at source-clip boundaries. | Directly motivated by the close temporal-encoding precedent and current data size. |
| Vision | Retain the same cached visual backbone and soft-pooling setup across conditions; allow the existing visual projection to learn. | Isolate the effect of bodily information before changing the visual backbone. |
| Fusion | A modest learned fusion of visual features and the body representation, producing a representation scored against language. Body content must reach that representation, not only a region-selection coefficient. | Lets the hypothesis be tested directly. A large transformer is not required initially. |
| Language | Keep the same weak speech, vocabulary, and matching objective; do not provide referent IDs or force held-object correspondence. | Avoid granting the body condition cleaner supervision. |
| Missing body | Explicitly train the visual-only path as well as the combined path, with a recognizable missing-modality state. | Supports picture-only testing, though it does not guarantee successful transfer. |
| Sensory objective | Test one masked body-prediction objective as a separate addition to direct fusion; score it on held-out clips against persistence/mean baselines. | Distinguishes lack of body information from lack of a learning signal. |
| Transfer objective | Add a separately evaluated teacher/auxiliary transfer objective only if combined-input competence is demonstrated and visual-only performance lags. | Makes a failed result interpretable and avoids bundling too many changes. |

The proposed sensory objective should not rely on labels or privileged mesh information. Predicting masked body measurements uses supervision that comes from the stream itself. If a latent prediction objective is selected later, prevent collapse with a suitable fixed/EMA target and variance or contrastive safeguards; that additional machinery is not automatically necessary for a small initial raw-signal prediction test.

Do not silently narrow the main question to training-time supervision only: a body branch is an actual learner input in the combined condition. The visual-only test measures retention/transfer from that experience, while a combined-input diagnostic determines whether fusion itself works. Picture-only and interaction-based evaluations must be reported separately because their input conditions differ.

**Controls that would make the result interpretable**

1. **Video-only, matched learner.** Same visual features, language exposure, development selection, and optimization opportunity. Architectural changes such as soft pooling belong in every condition.
2. **Aligned body.** Real synchronized bodily inputs, with the proposed temporal encoding and fusion.
3. **Shuffled body, same architecture.** Permute whole valid sequences across episodes so their internal dynamics remain intact. Prefer matching coarse contact/motion statistics to avoid an obviously out-of-distribution control. State exactly what alignment the shuffle destroys.
4. **Existing attention-only body model.** Retain its result as the mechanism comparison: does body content help beyond location guidance? It need not become a second canonical implementation maintained indefinitely.
5. **Signal and temporal ablations, after an effect appears.** Contact versus posture; ordered sequences versus pooled/shuffled time; and the sensory objective off versus on. These identify the source of a gain rather than merely adding experiments.

Test generalization by object instance, subject, and original source-clip family; repetitions or augmented descendants of a clip must stay together. Grouping by newly generated session IDs alone can miss replay dependence. Audit word-to-source-motion assignments and grasp-fit quality: a category can correlate with the original motion template even after its visible mesh is replaced. This review identified a risk from the construction, not a demonstrated leak in a particular completed run.

Use paired seeds and paired effect estimates, not just overlapping standard-deviation bars. Preserve a final held-out test for confirmation after method development. If test performance repeatedly guides architecture choices, it becomes exploratory evidence; another independent confirmation is needed for a final scientific claim.

Held versus seen-only words remain useful subgroup reports, but they are not randomized controls: frequency, difficulty, and available instances differ. Also, a better shared representation could improve seen-only words indirectly. Neither “no effect on seen-only words” nor a rigid oracle ≥ body ordering should be treated as a mathematical requirement. Oracle results are conditional on how the oracle is implemented and trained.

**How to read the next outcomes**

| Observation | What it would mean | Next decision |
|---|---|---|
| Body representations fail even basic held-out sensory probes | Information, encoding, objective, or data quality remains insufficient. | Inspect geometry, timing, boundary handling, and signal preservation before a larger learner. |
| Sensory probes work, but aligned and shuffled body give the same grounding performance | Body content may be irrelevant to the task or poorly fused; representation quality alone is insufficient. | Check weak alignment and whether labels distinguish properties present in the body stream. |
| Aligned body improves combined-input grounding, but picture-only performance is unchanged | Fusion works; retention/transfer is the remaining problem. | Test one explicit transfer mechanism while keeping the teacher/data fixed. |
| Picture-only benefit occurs only with a frozen visual backbone | Body may compensate for an architectural visual bottleneck. | Report this conditional result; later compare a stronger visual learner with matched resources. |
| Benefit appears mainly at small data sizes | Body improves acquisition efficiency. | Treat it as useful progress, then measure whether the difference persists with more data. |
| Benefit persists over increasing data after both learners are adequately optimized | Evidence toward the intended operational ceiling claim. | Confirm across seeds, held-out variations, and uncertainty in learning-curve fits. |

**Practical reading order**

Read Zambelli first for a directly usable body encoder; Thomason for the closest explicit comparison of language grounding with and without nonvisual senses; Ngiam for missing-modality transfer; Lee for sensory predictive objectives; and FactorCL for the danger of discarding modality-specific information. Read the Tani-group work next for longer-term joint language–sensorimotor development. TVL, UniTouch, Sparsh, and recent tactile policies are valuable design references, but their pretrained semantics, sensor hardware, or downstream tasks should not be imported as if they already solve Nursery's question.

**Recommended decision**

Proceed toward a temporal, information-bearing body pathway using the signals already recorded. Revise the scientific interpretation of the leakage checks before deciding which features to retain. Establish combined-input competence, then test transfer into the visual learner. Keep rich physics, broad vocabulary changes, large transformers, and additional pretrained semantic models as separately justified extensions.
