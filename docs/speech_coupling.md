# Caregiver naming vs. infant handling: literature estimates for the Step 2 speech generator

Labels: **[V]** = verified, I opened the full text (PMC or publisher/author PDF) and read the number. **[S]** = from an abstract or secondary source, not checked in the paper. **[D]** = my own arithmetic on verified numbers (not reported by the authors). "Not found" means I looked and could not locate it.

Main finding: no paper I could open reports P(name | infant holds object) vs P(name | visible, not held) as a per-object probability. The usable evidence is (a) the share of naming time/events where the referent is held, (b) relative rate ratios, (c) naming rates per minute. Papers that likely have the exact split (Yu & Smith 2012 Fig. 7, West & Iverson 2017, Suarez-Rivera et al. 2022, Tamis-LeMonda et al. 2019) were paywalled or only in figures.

## Not found

- Event-level P(name | held by infant) and P(name | visible, not held) as per-object probabilities: not reported in any paper I could open.
- Percent of namings with referent held by the *parent* only: not found. Yu & Smith 2012 report parent holding is not reliably linked to naming (Fig. 7 values not in text).
- Tamis-LeMonda et al. 2019 ("Routine language"): only the abstract was reachable. No object-name proportions found.
- Clerkin et al. 2017 (Phil Trans B): codes objects in view at mealtimes; it does not code naming or handling.
- Schroer & Yu 2022 Infancy, Mendez et al. 2024, Bergelson 2019 and Frank et al.: not opened.
- The Karmazyn-Raz & Smith 2022 OSF data were later downloaded and analyzed (see "Computed from data" below).

## Computed from data: Karmazyn-Raz & Smith (2022) OSF free-play set

**[computed from data]**, not published numbers. Source: https://osf.io/254yq/ ("Free Play", public project, 18 files, 0.36 MB, created 2022-01-13; local copy in `outputs/speech_data/osf_254yq/`, script `outputs/legacy/step2/scripts/osf254yq_coupling.py`). 12-month-olds, 16 dyads (sub16 missing), ~167 min total (~10.4 min each), 33 toy ids (~32 per session). Terms: the OSF node has **no license set** (node_license = None) and no wiki or readme, so no reuse terms are stated. Treat as research-use data, cite the paper, and do not redistribute or commit it.

Data format and limits: one hand code per **5-s bin** (0.2 Hz frame sampled at bin start: object id held in each of 4 hands, 0 = none). Each naming row is a bin in which a parent said a toy's name (596 naming events, 3.56 per min, matching the paper's 36 per 10 min). The hold state is a snapshot at the start of the bin, while the name can fall anywhere in the following 5 s. All timing is at 5-s resolution, and short touches between samples are missed. Blank/odd object ids were repaired by name (e.g. "burt" = id 10).

**1. Who holds the referent in the bin where it is named (n = 596)**

| State | Events | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Infant only | 104 | 0.174 |
| Parent only | 142 | 0.238 |
| Both | 32 | 0.054 |
| Neither | 318 | 0.534 |

Infant holds (alone or both): 0.228 (per-dyad mean 0.243, SD 0.141, range 0.07-0.62). Parent holds: 0.292. This is lower than Schroer & Yu's ~0.42 infant-held, as expected for 12 months and 5-s snapshots.

**1b. P(object named in a 5-s bin | state)**, per object-bin: infant holds it 0.126 (134/1062, ~1.5 names per min); only the parent holds it 0.214 (141/659, ~2.6 per min); held by neither 0.0049 over all 33 ids (318/64,543, ~0.06 per min) or 0.0080 restricted to objects handled by anyone that session (~0.10 per min). So a held object is named ~16-26x more often than an unheld one per bin, and parent-held objects are named more often than infant-held ones. Visible-but-unheld is not coded.

**2. Per-object naming rate, infant-handled vs never-handled** ("handled" = held in any sampled bin, 0.2 Hz, so it undercounts brief touches)

| Group | Names per object per min (mean over dyads, SD) |
|---|---|
| Objects infant held at least once | 0.231 (0.127) |
| Objects infant never held | 0.040 (0.027) |
| Objects nobody held | 0.021 (0.014) |

About 5.9x (infant-handled vs never-infant-held) and 11x (vs nobody-held). 23.0% of name events go to objects the infant never held in that session; 32.7% of distinct named (object, session) pairs were never infant-held, and 13.7% were never held by anyone. Mean names per named object per session: 1.85 for never-infant-held (median 1.5, n = 74) vs 3.02 for infant-held (median 2.0, n = 152). Over ~10.4 min, a never-held object that gets named gets ~2 names, so the 5-mention floor needs ~2.5x the session length per word, or pooling across sessions.

**3. Timing at 5-s resolution**

Probability the infant holds the named object, relative to the naming bin (base rate for an infant-handled object: 0.041):

| Lag from naming | -20 s | -15 s | -10 s | -5 s | 0 | +5 s | +10 s | +15 s | +20 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(infant holds referent) | 0.064 | 0.079 | 0.082 | 0.145 | 0.228 | 0.234 | 0.212 | 0.174 | 0.134 |

The peak is at naming and just after, elevated for about +-15 s.

Naming bin minus the nearest infant hold-onset bin of that referent (within +-30 s; 353 of 596 events have one): -10 s 14%, -5 s 20%, 0 s 28%, +5 s 18%, +10 s 5%; mean -1.7 s, median 0 s. 43% of names come **before** the hold begins, 51% fall 0-10 s after onset, 55% within 0-20 s. Among names where the infant is holding the referent (n = 136), 74% occur in the first bin of the hold run, 18% in the second (5-10 s in), and 93% within 10 s of hold onset.

Note this differs from the lab studies above: in free play with 12-month-olds, naming is about equally likely to lead the infant's grasp by up to 10 s as to follow it (Yu & Smith's 4-s hold-before-naming is from 17-20-month-olds with 3 toys). This agrees with Chang et al. 2016 (hands arrive after naming at 6-9 months).

## Suggested generator parameters

Ranges blend the rows above. Ages differ across sources (4 mo to 34 mo); GRAB-style handling of tabletop objects is closest to the 12-26 mo toy-play studies. Treat 1c-1e as the strength of coupling and sweep it, since sources disagree (1.0x at 4-9 mo handling, 1.9x for gaze, >7x at 18-23 mo touch).

| Knob | Range | Central | Source behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| p(name event refers to infant-held object) | 0.20 to 0.50 | 0.35 (0.23 at 12 mo, 0.42 at 17 mo+) | Schroer & Yu 2023 (0.36-0.48 by outcome; 0.42 weighted [D]); OSF data, 12 mo: 0.228 [computed]; Schroer 2024 0.26-0.28 whole-utterance |
| p(name event refers to parent-held, infant-not-holding object) | 0.15 to 0.30 | 0.24 | OSF data [computed]: 0.238 parent-only, 0.054 both. Include this class; it is as large as the infant-held class at 12 mo |
| p(name event refers to visible, not held object) | 0.10 to 0.25 | 0.16 (OSF "neither" = 0.53 is held-by-neither, includes unseen) | Schroer & Yu 2023 "visual alone" 0.15-0.18. Add the parent-held share if you generate parent-held objects (not found; suggest 0.05-0.15 from Suanda frame base rates and Karmazyn-Raz) |
| p(name event refers to object not in view / floor) | 0.10 to 0.40 | 0.25 | In-view: 0.76 (Suanda), 0.50-0.60 (Bergelson, 6 mo), 0.90 (Custode, [S]). Schroer & Yu "neither" 0.42 includes in-view but unattended objects |
| Per-object lift, held vs not held (sensitivity sweep) | 2x to 20x | 6x (infant-handled vs never-handled objects, per min) | 1.9x gaze (Chang), >7x touch ([S] Suarez-Rivera 2022), ~10x Topic vs Comment toys; OSF [computed]: 5.9x per object per min handled vs never-handled, 16-26x per 5-s bin held vs unheld |
| Naming offset window, name onset minus hold/touch onset | -10 s to +10 s (5-s resolution data); lab studies -2 to +5 s | centered on 0 to +5 s, with 30-40% mass before onset (OSF 43% before at 12 mo) | Yu & Smith: hold established ~4 s before and lasting 5 s after naming; unlearned 2.5 s before to 1.2 s after. Chang: infant hands more frequent after naming at 6-9 mo; 2 s contingency window. Schroer: +-3 s windows. Mean utterance 1.25-1.86 s, so require name span to overlap the hold |
| p(utterance names any concrete object) | 0.15 to 0.40 | 0.30 | Schroer 2024: 0.295 focal (object play), ~0.38 any concrete noun [D]; Chang: 0.07-0.16 at 4-9 mo |
| Naming events per minute (for seen-only budget) | 3 to 6 | 5 | 3.6 (12 mo), 5.2-5.3 (18-25 mo), 4.7-6.1 (25-34 mo); Chang 1.5-3.1 (4-9 mo) |

Seen-only words (named at least 5 times): OSF data add that 23% of naming events go to objects the infant never held, but a never-held named object gets only ~1.9 names per ~10 min. Natural data name rarely handled objects rarely (0.47 per 10 min per toy at 12 mo, which is ~0.05/min). So the 5-mention floor will not arise naturally in a short dataset. It has to be a generator constraint: sample seen-only names at a floor rate (about 1 in 6 to 1 in 4 name events, per the visual-only and in-view-not-held shares) and check the count per word, instead of trusting the human rates. Repetitions per name in published toy-play sessions are 2.5-9.7 over 6-10 minutes, so 5+ per word over a multi-hour dataset is well within the human range.
